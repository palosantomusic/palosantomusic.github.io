Bullerengue

Afro-Colombian · Caribbean Coast

Bullerengue is one of the oldest and most sacred Afro-Colombian musical traditions, born on the Caribbean coast of Colombia. Rooted in the spiritual and cultural practices of enslaved African communities, it is a music of resistance, memory and celebration — carried through generations by women's voices and the pulse of hand drums.

At its core, Bullerengue is a dialogue between the lead singer — the cantadora — and a chorus of voices responding in call and response. The rhythm is driven by two drums: the alegre and the tambora, alongside handclaps and maracas. There are no strings, no brass — only skin, wood and the human voice.

Palo Santo brings this living tradition to Berlin, honouring its roots while making space for it on European stages.

Cumbia

Afro-Indigenous · Caribbean Coast

Colombian Cumbia is the heartbeat of the Caribbean coast — a rhythm born from the encounter of African, Indigenous and Spanish cultures. Originally danced around bonfires on the banks of the Magdalena River, Cumbia became the most widely recognised Colombian folk music in the world, spreading across Latin America and evolving into dozens of regional forms.

In its traditional form, Cumbia is built on the interplay of the gaita flutes — female and male — the alegre, tambora and maracas. The dance is characterised by slow, swaying footwork and dramatic costumes of fire and colour. Palo Santo performs Cumbia in its original, acoustic form — faithful to the sound of the Colombian Caribbean coast.

Puya & Mapalé

Afro-Colombian · Caribbean Coast

Puya is the wild, fast-paced cousin of Cumbia — driven by the same instruments but at a frenetic tempo that pushes dancers and musicians to their limits. A rhythm of pure energy and joy, Puya is a staple of Barranquilla's Carnival and one of the most physically demanding styles in the Colombian folk tradition.

Mapalé is even more explosive — an Afro-Colombian rhythm with direct African roots, characterised by rapid, acrobatic movements and intense drumming. Originally performed at night after long days of labour on the Magdalena River, Mapalé is raw, powerful and unmistakable. Together, Puya and Mapalé bring the fire of Barranquilla's Carnival to wherever Palo Santo plays.
